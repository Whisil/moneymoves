# MoneyMoves

## What MoneyMoves Is

MoneyMoves is an independent financial storytelling channel and newsletter about the forces moving money through the world.

It follows the money behind public figures, governments, markets, technology, media, and real-world schemes. A MoneyMoves story should not stop at what happened. It should ask who benefited, how the system worked, where the money went, and what the polished public version leaves out.

The newsletter is a direct, researched guide to the forces behind financial stories. It is edited by people who remain responsible for what they publish. It does not sell passive-income fantasies, hidden subscriptions, or automated content dressed up as expertise.

## Audience

The core audience is Gen Z and younger millennial readers who:

- Are interested in money but do not identify with conventional finance media.
- Follow news, technology, politics, creators, markets, and internet culture together.
- Want the mechanism behind a story, not just its headline.
- Are skeptical of institutions, influencers, and easy explanations.
- Value strong visuals, direct language, and evidence they can inspect.

The reader should leave feeling more capable of recognizing incentives and tracing power—not merely entertained by a conspiracy-shaped story.

## Editorial Promise

MoneyMoves explains the systems behind the spectacle.

Every story should earn trust by separating documented facts, informed analysis, and open questions. The work can be provocative, but it should never manufacture certainty. A useful internal test is: “Can we show the reader how we know this?”

The current website promise is:

> Stories about money, power, schemes and the systems moving the world. Researched by people. Delivered free.

## Editorial Pillars

### Money

How wealth is made, moved, hidden, spent, and defended.

### Power

The financial incentives behind political figures, governments, institutions, and monopolies.

### Schemes

Fraud, arbitrage, loopholes, influence networks, and businesses whose real product differs from their public pitch.

### Markets

Important market shifts explained through people, incentives, and consequences rather than ticker commentary alone.

### Technology

AI, platforms, automation, and the economic changes they create—especially who captures the upside and who carries the risk.

### Media

The attention economy, information trust, creator businesses, recommendation systems, and the economics behind what people see.

## Voice

MoneyMoves is:

- **Curious:** begin with a real question and follow the evidence.
- **Skeptical:** interrogate convenient narratives, including our own.
- **Direct:** use plain language and strong verbs; remove finance theater.
- **Cinematic:** create momentum, contrast, and memorable transitions.
- **Evidence-led:** distinguish facts from inference and make uncertainty visible.
- **Human:** sound like a sharp person explaining what they found, not an institutional memo or an AI summary.

Use humor and provocation as pacing tools, not substitutes for proof. Avoid empty hype, guru language, moral panic, forced slang, and claims that are more certain than the reporting.

## Visual Language

The foundation is monochrome: near-black, warm white, high contrast, and oversized typography. Signal red is the recurring accent used for the fact, number, phrase, or interface state that demands attention. Cyan and magenta appear sparingly as chromatic interference rather than a general-purpose palette.

The core type system uses Peace Sans for major headlines, LT Superior Mono for body copy and interface labels, BBH Bartle for occasional editorial accents, and Press Start 2P for small pixel details. Brand assets include a transparent uppercase `MONEYMOVES` wordmark and square YouTube marks rendered directly from the supplied font files, with warm-white letterforms, red/cyan edge separation, and print-like distress.

Core treatments include:

- Bold grotesque typography, compressed scale, and dense editorial composition.
- Halftone and dither patterns that make footage feel printed, scanned, or reconstructed.
- RGB separation and chromatic aberration at image edges and during transitions.
- Short glitches, horizontal displacement, and signal-drop moments.
- Inversion, heat-map color, barrel distortion, and hard crops.
- Layered source material: documents, maps, interfaces, archival images, numbers, and captions.

Effects should communicate investigation, interference, or a change in the argument. They should not run continuously simply to decorate the frame.

### Legibility Rules

- Keep primary reading surfaces stable even when the surrounding scene moves.
- Use one dominant effect at a time.
- Preserve strong text/background contrast beneath grain and halftone layers.
- Keep glitches brief and avoid applying them to form controls while someone is interacting.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences and never rely on motion alone to communicate meaning.
- Treat signal red as emphasis; overuse makes every element equally loud.

## Website Direction

The first version is intentionally compact: logo, editorial promise, email signup, and one animated ASCII object. Desktop should read as a complete one-screen poster. Mobile may scroll to retain comfortable type and controls.

The rotating ASCII money loop is the only major animated element. It represents money and influence continuously circulating through systems without turning the page into a literal financial terminal.

Primary website copy:

- Eyebrow: “Free financial newsletter”
- Headline: “Follow the money.”
- Description: “Stories about money, power, schemes and the systems moving the world. Researched by people. Delivered free.”
- Call to action: “Join the list”
- Reassurance: “Free. No spam. Just the moves that matter.”

## Future Expansion

When there is enough real content to support it, the site can grow with:

- A latest-issue feature with one strong premise and a clear reading time.
- A searchable newsletter archive organized by editorial pillar.
- Compact story cards that show the central question rather than generic summaries.
- Source notes or research methodology for evidence-heavy investigations.
- A “Start here” collection for new readers.
- A transparent about page explaining authorship, corrections, sourcing, and AI-use policy.

Future sections should be added only when they improve trust or help readers reach published work. The signup should remain the clearest action on the landing page.
